# Ten Months, Ten Receipts

**I. January**
The year starts as a sentence with no verb.
Nobody funds a maybe, nobody pays a perhaps —
write it down anyway; the receipt has to start somewhere.

**II. February**
Doubt arrives wearing your own coat.
*Who asked you?* Nobody asked you.
Correct. Wanting it is the whole application.

**III. March**
You build the thing badly, then again, less badly.
The first proof is ugly and true.
Truth does not require a design system.

**IV. April**
Photograph it: a bolt, a page, a broken build,
a number that finally moved when you pushed.
*Pics or it didn't happen* isn't mockery — it's a file format for trust.

**V. May**
Post it where strangers can open it.
A link is a door with no bouncer.
If they can't check it, you didn't publish — you hoped.

**VI. June**
Someone reads it and puts money behind the reading.
Not a grant committee: one person, one decision,
one escrow contract holding a promise in wei.

**VII. July**
Others arrive with proofs of their own.
You learn the odd arithmetic of this place:
a bounty is not a job — it's a market in effort.

**VIII. August**
Then you wait. Waiting is the unpaid half of the work.
The creator judges, the voters weigh, the market clears or it doesn't.
You still have the proof, and the proof is yours either way.

**IX. September**
The balance moves — small, specific, on-chain,
minus two and a half percent for the house.
Withdraw it yourself. Nobody wires money to the polite.

**X. October**
Ten months of ideas turned into small, paid facts.
The lesson is narrow and it survives contact with use:
coordination is not a feeling. It is a receipt a stranger can read.
